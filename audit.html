Audit · Hostile review of this study

Fourteen claims audited, none found wrong, four found soft

Conducted under a standard where unverifiable claims are downgraded rather than given benefit of the doubt.

1. Audit summary

Ready to ship after corrections

This is a hostile review of the study published on this site, conducted against the standard that unsupported specifics are false by default and unverifiable claims are downgraded rather than given benefit of the doubt. Fourteen significant claims were audited: the headline numbers, the population construction, the methodological choices, and the factual premises the study rests on.

No claim was found to be wrong. That is a weaker result than it sounds, because the study already caught and documented three substantial errors during construction — a mis-specified comparator that produced a 47-point artifact, a signing-club field derived from a proxy that broke, and three verified non-tryouts counted as converts because verification findings were never written back to the data. Those are disclosed in the repository and on the methods page.

The headline finding is reproducible and the sensitivity analysis is real. The exposure is not in the arithmetic. It is in the population: whether 43 is the right set of players. Five of seven camp opening dates are estimates, several tryout lists are curated to notable players, and two players in the analysis have unresolved tryout status. Every one of those weaknesses runs in the same direction, toward a population that may be incomplete or slightly wrong at the edges.

The single highest-risk input is camp opening dates. They set the boundary that determines which players are candidates at all, and five of seven are estimated rather than sourced.

3. Patterns

Four patterns across the findings

Every completeness claim depends on third-party curation

The population rests on annual tryout lists published by news outlets. Several state openly that they cover notable players. The study cannot distinguish an unlisted tryout from no tryout, so completeness is asserted rather than demonstrated in every season. This is one systemic weakness, not several independent ones.

Methodological thresholds were set after seeing their effects

The roster threshold moved from five games to ten after the five-game version excluded three verified converts. The candidate window widened after the narrow version missed two. Both changes are defensible and both are disclosed, but both were made with the data in view. A reader should treat the specification as partly data-dependent.

Errors were caught internally, never externally

Three substantial errors were found and corrected during construction. All three were caught by the author. No external party has reproduced this. Self-audit has a known ceiling, and the pattern of the errors — a wrong covariate, a broken key, an unpropagated filter — is the kind that recurs.

Sensitivity testing covers the matching, not the population

Nine specifications vary how comparators are chosen. None varies who counts as a convert. Given that population construction carries the larger risk, the sensitivity analysis tests the better-established half of the study.

4. What was not audited

Scope

  • Individual rows were not re-verified. Fifty-four tryout confirmations are accepted as cited without independently re-checking each source.
  • The scripts were not re-executed or code-reviewed. Computed figures are accepted as correctly calculated from the stated inputs.
  • Comparator selection was not independently reproduced. The 128 matched players were not checked individually for tryout status, so a comparator who was in fact on a tryout would not have been detected here.
  • The statistical approach was not assessed. Confidence intervals use a normal approximation, which is questionable at these sample sizes and was not evaluated.
  • Outcome resolution was not spot-checked against an independent source such as a reference site.

5. Recommended next steps

What to do about each finding

Correct now

  • Add a sensitivity result excluding Ben Hutton and Max McCormick, whose tryout status is unresolved. Two of 43 could move the point estimate by more than a point.
  • State the 43 as a lower bound wherever the population is described, including on the findings page.

Verify before wider circulation

  • Source camp opening dates for all seven seasons. This is the highest-value remaining verification and it is bounded work.
  • Resolve Yannick Weber's signing club and Mark Letestu's tryout club. Neither moves a number; both are loose threads a reader could pull.
  • Spot-check a sample of comparators for tryout status. A tryout player sitting in the comparator group would bias the result toward zero.

Re-audit later

  • Re-resolve the 2024-25 cohort once 2025-26 is complete. Nine converts, one seventh of the sample, currently resolve against a season in progress.
  • Recheck published source URLs before any republication. Two sources used here have already gone offline or been overwritten during this project.

Add caveats

  • Note on the findings page that the sensitivity analysis varies the matching and not the population.
  • Note that confidence intervals use a normal approximation at small n.

6. Verdict

Ready to ship after corrections

The underlying work is sound. The headline is reproducible from published scripts, the comparator is specified correctly after an earlier version was found wrong and replaced, and the sensitivity range is genuinely stable.

The corrections required are two: publish a sensitivity result excluding the two unresolved players, and describe the population as a lower bound rather than a count. Both are small and neither threatens the finding.

The honest limit is this. The study demonstrates that its 43 identified converts survived at a lower rate than matched comparators. Whether those 43 are the right 43 rests on tryout lists that no longer have a complete source behind them. That is stated on the methods page, and it is the correct thing for a reader to remain uncertain about.
