The finding

Making the team on a tryout is not the comeback it looks like

Every September a handful of veterans show up to an NHL camp with no contract. They are on a professional tryout: permission to practise and play preseason games, nothing more. A few earn a deal and make the roster. Those are the feel-good stories of the preseason.

This study followed 43 of them across seven seasons and asked a simple question. A year later, are they still in the league?

Just under half were. Comparable players who made the same rosters without a tryout survived at roughly seven in ten. The gap is about 20 points.

That comparison is the whole point. A survival rate on its own says nothing, because these are marginal players and marginal players wash out. The question is whether they wash out faster than similar players who did not need a tryout to get there. They do.

What counts as a convert

Three conditions, all of them strict

  • On a professional tryout in an NHL training camp.
  • Signed a standard contract with that same club on or before its opening night.
  • Played in at least one of that club's first ten games.

The third condition matters more than it sounds. Signing a contract and making the team are different outcomes. Three verified tryout converts in this set signed a deal and never appeared for the club at all.

Players who signed after opening night are excluded, as are players who tried out with one club and signed with another. Both are real and both are a different story from the one this study is about.

The full definitions, the identification method, and everything that went wrong along the way are on the methods page. The study was also put through a hostile review; that is on the audit page.

The definition is also narrower than the phenomenon. Scott Harrington converted with San Jose in 2022-23, did not survive, and turned up on a tryout in 2024-25 — two years later, so outside the count by construction.

Data

The dataset is published alongside the finding

CapFriendly held the only complete public record of NHL tryouts and went offline in July 2024. Nothing replaced it retroactively. Rebuilding this required assembling candidates from contract timing and then verifying every one against published tryout coverage, season by season.

What came out of that is a set of 43 verified camp converts across seven seasons, each row carrying its tryout source, signing date, club, roster outcome and one-year outcome. As far as I can tell nothing equivalent exists publicly.

The dataset, the scripts that built it, and the full decision log are in the repository.
