Methods · Identification, matching and limits

How 43 converts were identified and matched

The data source this study needed was taken offline in 2024. This is how the population was rebuilt, and what went wrong along the way.

The problem

The source this study needed no longer exists

CapFriendly maintained the complete public database of NHL professional tryouts. It was bought by the Washington Capitals and taken offline in July 2024. Nothing replaced it retroactively.

What survives is fragmentary. Annual news trackers are either frozen mid-camp or curated to notable players. One of the best-structured historical trackers is now a parked domain for sale. NHL.com publishes a tryout page each season but overwrites it at the same URL. PuckPedia, the live successor, carries trackers only from 2025.

A single-source tryout list is not usable. Reconciling several still cannot prove completeness. So the study could not start from tryout lists at all.

Identification, step one

Contract timing as a signature

A player cannot appear in an NHL game without a contract on file. A tryout player arrives with none and must sign before he can play. So every convert has a signing date inside the camp window, and the population can be generated from contract data rather than tryout lists.

Filtering PuckPedia's contract records to standard-level deals signed by unrestricted free agents between camp opening and the club's opening night produces the candidate set. Restricted free agent signings drop out on signing status, which removes contract holdouts. Entry-level deals drop out on contract level, which removes teenage prospects.

Why the window is team-relative

Opening night varies by club, sometimes by more than a week. In 2022-23 two teams opened in Prague on October 7 while others started October 15. A single league-wide cutoff misclassifies players at the edges in both directions, so every candidate is measured against its own club's opener.

Where the window came from

Plotting standard free agent signings by date shows the same shape in every season: dense activity through July and August, a trough of two to four weeks, then a cluster resuming at camp. The trough appears in every season where the method applies and is documented explicitly in four of them, so the window boundary is observed rather than assumed. It is absent in 2020-21, for reasons covered below.

An earlier version of this study set the window at twelve days before the opener. That was too tight and missed two verified converts who signed within a week of camp opening. The window was widened to camp opening, which is why verification does more work at the early edge.

Identification, step two

Verification is part of identification, not cleanup

Contract timing generates candidates. It cannot identify converts, because two distinct things produce the same date pattern.

Paperwork timing

The signing date in contract databases is the central registry filing date, not the date terms were agreed. In 2021 the Islanders announced Zdeno Chara on September 18 and Zach Parise earlier still, yet both records show October 10, because the club filed both on the roster deadline. Contemporary coverage describes this as that general manager's standard practice.

Any club that files late produces candidates that look like conversions and are not. The distortion is invisible in the data.

Eve-of-camp signings

Players who agree to terms as camp opens sit on the same dates as genuine early conversions. Four candidates in this set turned out to be re-signings or straight free agent deals announced within days of camp opening.

Of the six candidates signing within days of camp opening across all seasons, two were genuine converts and four were not. The early edge of the window is where the method is weakest, and only a tryout source separates them.

Sources used

HFBoards annual training-camp threads record tryouts with their outcomes and were the most useful source found. NHL.com publishes an annual tryout page curated to notable players. Pro Hockey Rumors and Daily Faceoff run trackers of varying quality year to year. Every convert in the final set carries its source in the dataset.

Of 64 candidates, 54 were confirmed as tryouts with the signing club, seven were confirmed as something else and removed, and three could not be resolved either way, leaving 57 in the analysis. Two of those three unresolved cases carry into the final set, so 43 is a lower bound rather than a count.

The roster test

Ten games, and why not five

The intended definition was the opening-night active roster. The NHL API does not serve historical active rosters, so a proxy was needed.

The first version used the club's first five games. That excluded three verified converts who were healthy scratches in October, including one who went on to score 22 goals that season. All three were two or three days past the cutoff.

Ten games recovers all three and still excludes every minor-league assignment in the set. The four clearest exclusions hold at either threshold: their first appearances came in November, January and February.

This threshold was chosen after a first pass showed five was too tight. It is not a natural break in the data. A first look suggested a clean discontinuity at five games; widening to ten produced new marginal cases at the new boundary, which means the apparent gap was a property of where the line sat rather than structure in the data.

Survival at the one-year mark uses the same ten-game rule, so both ends of the study are measured identically.

The comparator

Matching, and the specification that was wrong first

Each convert is matched to three players who appeared in a club's first ten games that same season without a tryout, on position group exactly, age within two years, and prior-season NHL games played within fifteen.

The first specification produced a fake result

The original matching used position and age, with games played as a tiebreaker. It returned a differential of 47 points.

That number was an artifact. The comparators had a median of 82 games played and a minimum of 51 — every one a full-time regular. Two errors caused it. The tiebreaker sorted descending, so it selected the highest-games-played player available at each age. And the variable was games played during the season being matched, which is an outcome rather than a covariate: it selected players who went on to play a full season, then found that they survived.

The corrected specification uses the season before, as a constraint rather than a tiebreaker. Converts average 41.0 prior-season games, comparators 41.4, medians 46 and 46. Both groups are part-time NHLers from the previous year, which is the comparison the design called for.

Why age alone is not enough

At the ages these players cluster at, most others in the league are there because they are good. Matching on age without controlling for playing time compares marginal players to the league average, and a large gap between those groups is close to tautological.

Season notes

2020-21 is not like the others

That season opened in January 2021. Camps opened December 31 for the seven clubs that had not participated in the previous summer's return to play, and January 3 for the rest. There was a training camp. There were no preseason games.

Since a tryout permits camp and exhibition play only, conversions that season happened on practice sessions alone, with no games to showcase in. That is a difference in kind, not degree.

The contract signature also fails there. Free agency ran late into camp, so no trough exists and normal signings cannot be separated from conversions by date. That cohort was hand-assembled from the NHL.com tryout list, which records fifteen tryouts and their outcomes. Being curated to notable players, its completeness is lower than the others.

Taxi squads

Four to six player taxi squads existed only in that season. An earlier version counted taxi squad assignment as making the roster. That was reversed once the run showed it admitted five of nine converts in that cohort on a standard no other cohort faced, which would have inflated the count and depressed the survival rate for structural reasons.

Limits

What this study cannot tell you

  • Camp opening dates are sourced for two seasons of seven. The rest are estimates, and they determine which candidates enter the set. This is the largest unverified input.
  • Completeness cannot be proven. Several annual tryout lists cover notable players only, so an unlisted tryout cannot be ruled out in any season.
  • Signing dates are filing dates. Verification catches the cases it can reach; it cannot catch a club that filed late and was never written about.
  • Three candidates are unresolved. Two carry into the analysis with their tryout status unknown.
  • Signing club was derived from the signing general manager field. That is a proxy and it broke once, when a general manager changed clubs mid-study. Affected rows were corrected and are flagged in the dataset.
  • Cohort-level rates are not findings. Cohorts run from two to ten players. The spread between seasons is noise.
  • The sample does not support more than this. A regression with covariates and multi-year survival curves were both planned and both dropped. With 43 converts neither would be interpretable.
